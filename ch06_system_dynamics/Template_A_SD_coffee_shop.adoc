= Coffee Shop Example: Completed SD Template A

== 1. Business Question

*Primary:* If we invest $15,000 in upgraded espresso equipment, how does that
affect customer base and monthly revenue over 24 months? At what point does the
investment pay for itself?

*Secondary:* What happens if we raise prices by 10% simultaneously to offset
the investment cost?

== 2. Time Horizon and Time Unit

[options="header"]
|===
|Setting |Value |Notes

|Model time unit |months |Every flow is units/month; every delay constant is in months
|Horizon |24 months |
|Reporting interval |0.1 month |Integration is continuous; this is the output grid
|Conversion factor |1 month = 4.33 weeks |Apply to anything stated in weeks before it reaches the solver
|Discontinuity times |month 1.0, month 1.1 |Equipment step and price step land at 1.0; the investment pulse closes at 1.1
|===


Declaring the discontinuity times matters more than it looks. A solver with
adaptive stepping will happily step over a one-time event and miss it entirely.

== 3. Reference Behavior

[options="header"]
|===
|Observed quantity |Historical value |Source |Baseline must reproduce

|Customer base |380 to 420, flat for 18 months |Loyalty card actives |Stays within 10% of 400 through month 24
|Monthly revenue |$8,500 to $9,200 |POS monthly totals |$8,500 to $10,000
|Net cash flow |+$500 to $800/month |Bank statements |Positive, under $1,000/month
|Customer churn |6% to 8% of actives per month |Loyalty card lapse |Acquisition and churn within 2/month of each other at t = 0
|===


*Intended baseline mode:* flat to mildly growing. If the baseline grows past
500 customers or collapses below 350, the parameters are wrong, not the
structure.

== 4. Assumptions

No competitor response. Stable demographics. No external shocks. Equipment
improves service quality immediately; reputation lags. The shop already runs at
its maximum staffing of 4 baristas at t = 0, so any growth in the customer base
raises utilization rather than triggering a hire.

[options="header"]
|===
|Variable / Driver |Treatment |Why

|customer_base, brand_reputation, cash_reserves, satisfaction, word_of_mouth, churn_fraction, acquisition_rate, capacity, staff_fte |Endogenous |Inside the R1/B1/B2 loops; this is what the model is built to explain
|base demand rate, equipment investment timing and amount, price level, staff_experience |Exogenous |Inputs the model takes as given or varies per scenario

|===


== 6. Stocks

[options="header"]
|===
|Stock |Initial Value |Units |Initial value basis |Description

|customer_base |400 |customers |Measured (loyalty actives) |Active customers visiting at least once/month
|brand_reputation |76.5 |index 0 to 100 |Structural (set to its own equilibrium so the baseline starts at rest) |Lagged indicator of perceived quality
|cash_reserves |25,000 |USD |Measured (bank balance) |Available operating cash
|===


`brand_reputation` is driven toward `reputation_target = 100 × satisfaction`, and
baseline satisfaction at t = 0 is 0.765. Initializing the stock anywhere else
makes it climb or fall on its own, which shows up in the results as growth the
policy didn't cause. The owner's survey proxy put reputation nearer 55, and
running the model from 55 pushes the baseline to 449 customers by month 24 and
net cash to $1,222/month, both outside the reference behavior in section 3. The
equilibrium value is the defensible choice, and marking it *Structural* keeps
the assumption visible in Template C.

== 8. Auxiliary Variables

[options="header"]
|===
|Variable |Units |Depends On |Description

|staff_capacity |customers/month |staff_fte, customers_per_fte |Throughput the current staffing supports
|equipment_capacity |customers/month |equipment upgrade state |Throughput the machines support
|capacity |customers/month |min(staff_capacity, equipment_capacity) |The binding constraint of the two
|utilization |dimensionless |customer_base, capacity |customer_base / capacity
|wait_time_pressure |index 0 to 1 |utilization |0 when waits are negligible, 1 when waits drive customers away
|service_quality_index |index 0 to 1 |equipment_level, staff_experience |Weighted blend of equipment and barista skill
|perceived_value |index 0 to 1 |price_change |Falls as price rises above baseline; 1.0 at baseline price
|satisfaction |index 0 to 1 |service_quality_index, wait_time_pressure, perceived_value |Feeds both word of mouth and churn
|word_of_mouth |leads/month |customer_base, satisfaction |Raw lead generation, before delay and conversion
|acquisition_from_wom |customers/month |smoothed word_of_mouth, conversion_rate |Output of the SMOOTH
|base_acquisition_effective |customers/month |base_acquisition, brand_reputation |Walk-in acquisition, scaled by reputation
|retention_effect |dimensionless |satisfaction, retention_strength |Clipped adjustment to churn
|churn_fraction |1/month |satisfaction, price_change |Monthly churn as a fraction of customer_base
|reputation_target |index 0 to 100 |satisfaction |What reputation converges toward
|desired_fte |FTE |customer_base, target_load_per_fte |Staffing the manager wants at current demand
|===


*Index anchoring.* `equipment_level` 0 means equipment that fails mid-shift, 1
means best in class for the segment. `brand_reputation` 0 means actively avoided,
100 means the best reviewed shop in town. `satisfaction`, `perceived_value`, and
`wait_time_pressure` all run 0 to 1 with the endpoints given in the table above.

=== Capacity

[options="header"]
|===
|Parameter |Value |Units |Basis |Plausible range

|equipment_capacity_base |520 |customers/month |Measured (peak throughput observed) |480 to 560
|equipment_capacity_upgraded |700 |customers/month |Vendor specification |640 to 760
|customers_per_fte |130 |customers/month/FTE |Measured (payroll against traffic) |110 to 150
|target_load_per_fte |100 |customers/month/FTE |Policy (manager's staffing rule) |85 to 120

|===


At 400 customers the staffing rule already asks for 4 FTE, so the shop opens the
run at its ceiling. That makes the maximum capacity 4 × 130 = 520 customers/month
for every scenario, which is worth noting now: the equipment upgrade raises
equipment throughput to 700, but staff capacity caps at 520, so the upgrade's
throughput half never binds.

== 10. Nonlinear Relationships

[options="header"]
|===
|Relationship |Form |Anchor points |Basis

|wait_time_pressure(utilization) |Linear ramp, clipped to [0, 1] |0.75 → 0.0, 0.925 → 0.5, 1.10 → 1.0 |Structural; threshold set where queue complaints begin in review text
|perceived_value(price_change) |Linear decline, clipped to [0, 1] |0.0 → 1.0, +0.10 → 0.80, +0.50 → 0.0 |Estimated
|retention_effect(satisfaction) |Linear in (satisfaction − 0.60), clipped to [−0.5, +0.6] |0.35 → −0.2, 0.60 → 0.0, 0.85 → +0.2 |Structural; clip prevents churn from going negative
|base_acquisition_effective(brand_reputation) |Proportional to reputation / 76.5 |38.3 → 0.5×, 76.5 → 1.0×, 100 → 1.31× |Structural
|===


== 11. Constraints and Ceilings

[options="header"]
|===
|Constraint |Rule |Rationale

|Staffing ceiling |staff_fte ≤ 4 |Floor space behind the bar caps hiring
|Capacity is the binding limit |capacity = min(staff_capacity, equipment_capacity) |Buying throughput you can't staff buys nothing
|Non-negativity |customer_base ≥ 0, capacity ≥ 1 |Prevents division by zero and nonsense states
|Index bounds |satisfaction, perceived_value, wait_time_pressure ∈ [0, 1] |Clip after every computation
|Churn floor |churn_fraction > 0 |The retention clip in section 10 enforces this
|===


The staffing ceiling is what gives loop B1 something to push against. With the
ceiling at 4 FTE, capacity holds at 520 while the customer base grows, so
utilization climbs and the congestion brake engages. Raise the ceiling to 5 and
the staffing rule tracks demand closely enough that utilization barely moves, B1
goes quiet, and the model returns a growth trajectory with no brake on it.

== 12. Feedback Loops

----
R1 (Growth engine): customer_base → satisfaction → word_of_mouth →
   (SMOOTH) → acquisition_rate → customer_base, reinforced by
   satisfaction → brand_reputation → base_acquisition_effective
   Plain language: More satisfied customers bring more customers.

B1 (Capacity constraint): customer_base → utilization →
   wait_time_pressure → satisfaction → churn_rate → customer_base
   Plain language: Growth increases congestion, which pushes customers
   away. The natural brake.

B2 (Price sensitivity): price_change → perceived_value → satisfaction
   → churn_rate, plus a direct multiplier on churn_fraction
   Plain language: A management lever that weakens R1 in exchange for
   margin.
----

*Expected loop dominance.*

[options="header"]
|===
|Phase |Expected dominant loop |Why

|Months 0 to 6 |R1 |Quality improvement lands immediately; utilization is still near 0.77 and congestion is mild
|Months 6 to 18 |R1, weakening |Word of mouth compounds while utilization climbs toward the 520 ceiling
|Months 18 to 24 |B1 at parity |The customer base presses against the 520 capacity ceiling and growth flattens
|===


If the run doesn't show B1 gaining on R1 by month 24, either the capacity
parameters are too generous or the congestion ramp starts too late.

== 14. Equations

Model time unit: months. All flows are units/month; all SMOOTH constants are in
months. Every symbol below resolves to a stock, an auxiliary from section 8, or
a parameter from section 9.

----
staff_capacity        = staff_fte × customers_per_fte
capacity              = max(min(staff_capacity, equipment_capacity), 1)
utilization           = customer_base / capacity
wait_time_pressure    = clip((utilization − 0.75) / 0.35, 0, 1)

service_quality_index = w_equipment × equipment_level
                      + w_staff × staff_experience

perceived_value       = clip(1 − value_sensitivity × price_change, 0, 1)

satisfaction          = clip(w_service × service_quality_index
                        + w_wait × (1 − wait_time_pressure)
                        + w_value × perceived_value, 0, 1)

word_of_mouth         = customer_base × satisfaction × wom_coefficient
acquisition_from_wom  = SMOOTH(word_of_mouth × conversion_rate, wom_delay)

base_acquisition_effective = base_acquisition
                           × (brand_reputation / reference_reputation)
                           × demand_multiplier

acquisition_rate      = base_acquisition_effective + acquisition_from_wom

retention_effect      = clip(retention_strength
                        × (satisfaction − satisfaction_reference), −0.5, 0.6)

churn_fraction        = base_churn
                      × (1 + price_sensitivity × price_change)
                      × (1 − retention_effect)

churn_rate            = customer_base × churn_fraction

revenue_inflow        = customer_base × visits_per_month × avg_transaction
                        × (1 + price_change)
expense_outflow       = fixed_costs
                      + variable_cost_fraction × revenue_inflow
                      + investment_rate

reputation_target     = 100 × satisfaction
desired_fte           = clip(customer_base / target_load_per_fte, 0.5, max_fte)
----

Stock equations:

----
d(customer_base)/dt    = acquisition_rate − churn_rate
d(brand_reputation)/dt = (reputation_target − brand_reputation) / reputation_delay
d(cash_reserves)/dt    = revenue_inflow − expense_outflow
d(staff_fte)/dt        = (desired_fte − staff_fte) / hiring_delay
d(wom_smoothed)/dt     = (word_of_mouth × conversion_rate − wom_smoothed) / wom_delay
----

== 15. Exogenous Inputs and Policy Timing

[options="header"]
|===
|Input |Shape |Timing |Magnitude |What it changes

|Equipment investment (cash) |Pulse, width 0.1 month |Month 1.0 |$15,000 total, so investment_rate = $150,000/month across the pulse |expense_outflow spikes, then returns
|Equipment upgrade (capability) |Step, permanent |Month 1.0 |equipment_level 0.60 → 0.90; equipment_capacity 520 → 700 |service_quality_index and capacity
|Price increase |Step, permanent |Month 1.0 |+10% |revenue multiplier, perceived_value, churn_fraction
|demand_multiplier |Constant |All months |1.0 in deterministic runs |Hook for the stochastic runs in section 16
|===


*Solver note for Template B:* integrate in three segments, 0 to 1.0, 1.0 to
1.1, and 1.1 to 24, so both steps and both pulse edges land on segment
boundaries. Inside the pulse segment hold `max_step` to 0.025 month, which gives
the solver four evaluations across the 0.1 month pulse.

== 16. Uncertainty Specification

*Sampled in the stochastic runs:*

[options="header"]
|===
|Parameter |Distribution |Rationale

|wom_delay |Uniform(4, 8) weeks |The 6 week figure is a midpoint, not a measurement
|demand_multiplier |Lognormal(0, 0.15) |Month to month demand noise
|base_churn |Normal(0.07, 0.008) |Measured, but with real sampling variation
|conversion_rate |Uniform(0.18, 0.32) |Estimated, wide
|===


*Sobol bounds for global sensitivity* (nine parameters, total-order indices):
base_acquisition [12, 28], target_load_per_fte [85, 120], customers_per_fte
[110, 150], wom_coefficient [0.03, 0.10], conversion_rate [0.18, 0.32],
base_churn [0.055, 0.085], price_sensitivity [0.8, 2.5], wom_delay [0.92, 1.85]
months, retention_strength [0.5, 1.1].

The three capacity and demand parameters at the front of that list are there
because the OAT sweep ranks them first, second, and fourth. An earlier version of
this spec sampled only the six behavioral parameters and missed the strongest
driver in the model.

*Held fixed and why:* the financial parameters all come from POS and bank
records, so they carry little uncertainty relative to the behavioral ones. The
structural weights stay fixed because sampling them would test the structure
rather than the parameters, which is a Template C question.
