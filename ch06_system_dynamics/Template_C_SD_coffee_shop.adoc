== Section 9: Results Summary

*One-Paragraph Finding*

----
The equipment upgrade grows the customer base from 400 to 447 over two
years but does not pay for itself in that window. At month 24 the shop
is $4,598 behind where it would have been without the purchase.
Growth is front-loaded and stops after month 18, because the shop runs
out of baristas before it runs out of demand: four staff cap throughput
at 520 customers a month while the new equipment could serve 700.
Adding the 10% price increase pays back at month 22.6 but costs 32
customers, because price reaches churn within weeks while the quality
improvement takes two to three months to reach new customers.
----

*Key Numbers*

[options="header"]
|===
|Metric |Value |What It Means

|Best for customer base |A: Invest, 447 @ 24 mo |Highest growth, worst cash
|Best for cash |C: Invest + price, $18,909 |Only scenario that pays back in horizon
|Payback, Scenario A |None within 24 months |$10,402 of $15,000 recovered
|Payback, Scenario C |Month 22.6 |Margin arrives before customer loss
|Sensitivity risk |70% of runs show no payback in 24 months |Growth robust at 100%, payback is not
|Top driver, level |base_acquisition, ST 0.457 |Walk-in traffic, exogenous
|Top driver, timing |customers_per_fte, ST 0.656 |Barista productivity sets payback
|Policy resistance flag |Y |Scenario C, loop B2
|===


*Recommendation*

----
Treat this as a two-to-three year investment. The purchase grows the
customer base in 100% of sampled parameter combinations but clears its
cost inside 24 months in only 30%. If the money must return within two
years, pairing it with the 10% price increase is the only scenario that
gets there, at a cost of 32 customers and a weaker word-of-mouth
position in year three.

Price the staffing constraint before committing. Four baristas cap the
shop at 520 customers a month against equipment rated for 700, so about
a third of what the $15,000 buys cannot be used, and barista
productivity is the largest single driver of when payback lands.
----

*Limitations*

----
- No competitor response. base_acquisition is exogenous and the largest
  driver of the result.
- No seasonal variation.
- Utilization assumes a fixed 4.0 visits/customer/month.
- Two unmeasured structural parameters carry the conclusion: the
  wait-pressure ramp at utilization 0.75 and the brand_reputation
  initialization at 76.5.
- The word-of-mouth delay has no measurable effect on either output
  (Sobol total-order 0.000) despite three sections of specification.
- A fifth barista is not modeled. Raising max_fte to 5 reaches 492
  customers but omits the added payroll.
- Deterministic core. Variability appears only through the stochastic
  wrapper.
----
